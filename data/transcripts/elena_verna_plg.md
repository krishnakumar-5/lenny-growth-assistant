# Elena Verna on Product-Led Growth and Product-Led Sales

## Introduction: Why Retention Precedes Acquisition
Welcome to Lenny's Podcast. Today we are talking with Elena Verna, interim CMO at Miro, former head of growth at SurveyMonkey, and advisor to companies like Amplitude, Netlify, and MongoDB.

Elena explains: "The biggest mistake early-stage founders and product managers make is obsessing over acquisition before retention is solved. You cannot pour water into a leaky bucket. Product-led growth is fundamentally an operating model where the product drives acquisition, retention, and monetization. But retention is the absolute foundation. If your user retention curve does not flatten into a stable baseline, you do not have product-market fit, and spending marketing dollars is lighting capital on fire."

## The Distinction Between Freemium, Free Trial, and Reverse Trial
Lenny asks: "Elena, how should teams choose between freemium, free trials, and reverse trials?"

Elena breaks down the taxonomy:
"First, **Freemium** is best when your product has viral loops or high network effects, and where the marginal cost of serving a free user is near zero. Freemium is an acquisition mechanism, not just a pricing strategy.
Second, **Free Trial** is best when your value proposition requires time to realize, but once realized, users must pay. It creates urgency, usually 14 or 30 days.
Third, and my personal favorite for modern B2B, is the **Reverse Trial**. You give the user full enterprise access for 14 days without requiring a credit card. At the end of 14 days, instead of kicking them out of the product, you gracefully downgrade them to a permanently free tier. This builds product habit while demonstrating the power of paid enterprise features."

## Product-Led Sales (PLS) and Product-Qualified Leads (PQLs)
Elena continues:
"Product-Led Sales is not the opposite of PLG; it is the natural second engine of PLG. When you have thousands of individual users inside a company using your product self-serve, your sales team should not be cold-calling. They should be looking at Product-Qualified Leads (PQLs).
A PQL is defined by two vectors:
1. **Fit score:** Does the organization match your Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) in size, industry, and budget?
2. **Intent score:** Has the user or team hit high-intent product usage thresholds—such as inviting 5 teammates, hitting usage limits, or integrating with Slack or Salesforce?
When both fit and intent align, the sales team steps in to help the customer expand from a team deployment to an enterprise contract."
