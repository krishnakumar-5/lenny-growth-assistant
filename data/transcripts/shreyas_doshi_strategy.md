# Shreyas Doshi on Product Strategy and High Agency

## The Core Definition of High Agency
Lenny welcomes Shreyas Doshi, former product leader at Stripe, Twitter, and Yahoo, known across Silicon Valley for his foundational frameworks on product craft.

Lenny asks: "Shreyas, what does High Agency really mean in the context of product management?"

Shreyas responds:
"High agency is the ability to find a way to achieve what you want, regardless of the circumstances. A low-agency PM accepts constraints as immovable laws of physics. They say, 'We couldn't ship because legal said no,' or 'Design didn't have bandwidth.'
A high-agency PM treats constraints as puzzles to be solved. If legal says no, they sit down with legal, understand the exact underlying liability risk, and rewrite the proposal to mitigate the risk while preserving user value. High agency is not about being aggressive or bypassing rules; it is about relentlessly finding creative paths to positive outcomes."

## The Seven Principles of Product Strategy
Shreyas shares his playbook for defining strategy:
"Strategy is not a list of features or a roadmap. Strategy is a set of choices you make under conditions of uncertainty to achieve an advantage.
The three most critical components of any winning product strategy are:
1. **Diagnosis:** What is the true reality of the market, the user problem, and our core constraints? If you misdiagnose the problem, flawless execution will only help you fail faster.
2. **Guiding Policy:** What is our unique approach or posture to address that diagnosis? This is where trade-offs happen. A good strategy is defined by what you choose NOT to do.
3. **Coherent Actions:** What coordinated steps across product, engineering, and go-to-market will execute the guiding policy?"

## The Pre-Mortem: Killing Projects Before They Launch
Shreyas details the Pre-Mortem ritual:
"Before committing engineering resources to a major launch, bring the team together for a pre-mortem. Ask everyone: 'Imagine it is one year from today, and this product has completely failed. It was an embarrassing disaster. What caused it to fail?'
When you ask people to predict failure before it happens, you remove the social pressure to be polite. People speak freely about technical debt, missing distribution channels, and poor UX. You capture these failure modes upfront and engineer guardrails to eliminate them before writing code."
